# Approach: claim interleaved

**Descriptor:** hook `claim` · voices `interleaved` · shape `multi-loop` · language `plain` · ending
`joke-button` · format `narrated`.

Taken from a competitor's short cut from the same candy video as your first one: theirs had about 4× the
views (10k to 2.5k) at a similar age. The footage was the same; the approach was different.

**When it fits:** any video where the creator *reacts* along the way: tastings, tests, things breaking,
surprises. The more good real moments the video has, the better this approach works. If the creator barely
reacts until the end, prefer `question-recap`.

## Shape

The narrator sets up a moment, then **hands over to the real voice** for its payoff, again and again:

```
NARRATOR  claim hook                         ← states the extreme result, no question
NARRATOR  what it is, in plain words
NARRATOR  sets up a moment                   → REAL  the creator's reaction to it
NARRATOR  the next step, and what went wrong → REAL  the reaction
NARRATOR  the fix                            → REAL  "it worked"
NARRATOR  the next problem                   → REAL  the reaction
NARRATOR  the second attempt                 → REAL  the verdict
NARRATOR  the last setup (often "Then his friend tried one.")
REAL      the final reaction, then the joke button
```

Each narrator → real pair is a **micro-loop**: a small question opened by the narrator and closed by the real
voice within a few seconds. Four to six of them keep the viewer from swiping.

## Script rules

- **Hook: a claim, not a question.** State the extreme result as fact, in plain words, from the video's own
  promise (its title or the creator's stated goal), **in your own words**: "This guy made a candy that hits
  every taste at the exact same time." Line 1 starts with "This guy…".
- **Plain language.** Say what things *do*, not what they're called: "a powder twenty thousand times
  sweeter" becomes "the sweetest powder he could find"; "WS-23" becomes "ice cold"; "umami" becomes
  "savory". Keep a number only if it's instantly striking without context.
- **70–95 narrated words.** The real voices carry 15–25 seconds, so the narration is shorter than a recap.
  Never pad to reach a count.
- **Every narrator line leads into the next real line** or the next step. No line repeats what a real clip is
  about to say.
- **Write the set-up the person answers.** The narrator's line ends where the real line picks up, so the two read
  as one conversation: "This man says he's not scared of anything." → "I am not scared of anything." A "But…" line
  brings the setback, a "So he…" or "Somehow…" line the fix.
- **Cover the story, not just its highlights.** Walk through its stages in order, one punchy line each with its
  real number; a short that jumps from the start to the end loses viewers.
- **Never invent.** Every claim comes from the transcript, and every real line is quoted exactly from it.
- **Third person, short sentences**, numbers written as words.

## Choosing the real lines

- **4–6 inserts during the narration, plus 1–2 at the end.** Each is 1.5–7 seconds.
- **Short, emotional, and clear with no context:** "Right now, it's just pain." "It's like glue." "My gosh,
  it worked." "The spice just killed it." Avoid lines that need the long video to make sense.
- **Clean audio:** a line the viewer can understand at phone volume, without music or another person talking
  over it.
- **Never from an excluded range** (sponsor reads, ads, end screens).
- **The ending is a joke button when the video has one:** a short, understated real exchange after the
  payoff ("You can have the rest." / "Yeah, I'm okay, thanks."). Otherwise end on the strongest reaction.
- **Whole sentences only.** Each real line starts on the first word of a sentence and ends on the last word of
  one; never cut the person off mid-thought.
- **Give each one as exact source times** from the transcript's word timings, starting on the first word and
  ending just after the last.

## Example (the second candy short)

```
NARRATOR  This guy made a candy that hits every taste at the exact same time.
NARRATOR  Sweet, sour, salty, bitter, savory, spicy and ice cold, all in one bite.
NARRATOR  Before anything else, he tasted the spicy-cold mix by itself.
REAL      "Right now, it's just pain."
NARRATOR  Then every powder went into the boiling sugar, and the whole batch turned to goo.
REAL      "It's like glue."
NARRATOR  He fed it through his skull roller and hoped for the best.
REAL      "My gosh, it worked. It freaking worked. This is a miracle."
NARRATOR  One bite in, all he could taste was the spice.
REAL      "The spice just killed it."
NARRATOR  So batch two got way less of it.
REAL      "It literally is all the taste at the same time."
NARRATOR  Then his friend tried one.
REAL      "This is so nasty."
REAL      "You can have the rest." / "Yeah, I'm okay, thanks."
```

87 narrated words and about 22 seconds of real voices: 49.5 seconds in all.

After the payoff comes the closing question (see `story-arc`), never inside the beats above.

## The full-story version (the creator's best short)

The same shape, run over the whole video, made the creator's best-performing short ("10 Levels of Spicy"): every
stage of the challenge in order, 5–8 real lines answering the narrator, and the real payoff as one long continuous
take. Its narration is longer than this approach allows (150–220 words, 60–120 s), so it is its own approach; within
this one, take its habits: set-ups the person answers, every stage covered in a line, the strongest real ending.
