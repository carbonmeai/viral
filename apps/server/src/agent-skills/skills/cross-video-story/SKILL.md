# Shorts across several videos

The creator chose these videos together for a reason, and their brief says what it is. A cross-video short is
worth making only when **putting the videos side by side says something neither says alone**. If one video
holds the whole story, that's a single-video short, not a set one.

## The shapes

| Shape | What joins the videos | Hook that works | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Compilation** | The same kind of moment, again and again: every fail, every reaction, every "it worked" | The best instance first, then "and it happened again…" | `cut-down` (the creators' own lines, back to back) |
| **Comparison** | Two or more attempts at the same thing, judged | "Which one actually worked?" / the verdict up front | `question-recap`, `claim-interleaved` |
| **Multi-part story** | One story told across episodes: part 1 sets up, a later part pays off | The payoff from the last video, then how it started | `claim-interleaved`, `question-recap` |
| **Callback** | A claim or promise in one video, its proof or reversal in another | The contradiction: "He said X. Six months later…" | `claim-interleaved` |

Name the shape to yourself before proposing; each proposal's `why` says in a sentence what the videos add to
each other.

## Picking the videos for a proposal

- **Use the fewest videos that tell it.** Two strong videos beat five weak ones: every new video costs the viewer
  context. List only the videos the short really uses.
- **Every video earns its place** with at least one beat the short can't do without.
- **Order matters for multi-part stories and callbacks** (the earlier video sets up, the later one pays off);
  compilations and comparisons can open with whichever has the best moment.
- Different proposals may use different subsets of the set.

## Beats and quotes, per video

- **A beat lives in one video.** Its seconds are that video's own; never add videos' lengths together.
- **Quote a line from the video it's in,** word for word as that video's transcript has it, with the time it's
  said. The same words in two videos are two different quotes.
- **Excluded ranges are per video.** An ad in V2 at 300–360s says nothing about V1 at 300s.
- **Search before you claim a moment is missing.** With several long videos you may only have outlines:
  `search_transcript` finds a word or phrase across all of them, `read_transcript` reads a few minutes of one.

## Judging across videos

Score the radar as for any short (story-arc), plus two questions:
- **Does the second video change the meaning of the first?** If it only repeats it, drop it or pick another shape.
- **Would someone who never saw either video follow the jump?** A cut to a new video needs a line that says
  where we are now ("A year later, he tried again"). Count that cost against completion compulsion.
