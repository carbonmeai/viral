# Approach: question recap

**Descriptor:** hook `question` · voices `narration-then-reactions` · shape `single-arc` · language
`technical` · ending `reaction` · format `narrated`.

Reverse-engineered from a proven viral short: *"He Compressed 100 RedBulls into 1 Drink"* (60s), cut from
*"Making the world's most powerful Red Bull"* (42 min).

- **A new third-person voiceover, not the creator's audio.** "What would happen if you compressed 100 cans
  of Red Bull into one drink? This guy bought 100 cans…". One punchy sentence per story beat, with real
  numbers from the video (25 liters, 150 degrees, 66 cans).
- **It follows the long video in order**, compressing it about 40×: setup → machine → weigh-in → setback
  ("a third evaporated") → result → reaction.
- **The narration runs without interruption**; the creator's real voice comes in only at the end.
- **It ends on the creator's real reaction with the original audio** ("Whoa, that's crazy. Crazy good."),
  which closes the loop the hook opened.

Arc mapping (see `story-arc`): hook = "What would happen if…?" · rising action = setup and process ·
conflict = the setback · comeback = "somehow it worked" / the fix · payoff = the real reaction clip.

**When it fits:** build, experiment or process videos where the numbers are impressive in themselves and
the best real reaction comes at the very end.

## Script rules

- **110–130 words.** That gives about 45s of narration after speeding the voice up; the reaction clip
  brings the short to 50–58s. When the source can't support that many words without inventing, write fewer.
- **Line 1 is the open-loop question.** ("What would happen if…?")
- **Line 2 starts "This guy…"** and names the goal in plain words.
- **Every claim and number comes from the transcript.** Never invent.
- **The setback gets a "But…" line**, and the fix gets a "So he…" or "Somehow…" line.
- **Write numbers as words** ("twenty thousand"), so the voice reads them naturally.
- Third person throughout. Short sentences. No filler, no sign-off.
- **The ending:** one to three real reaction clips after the last narrated line (see `narrated-shots`), each on
  whole sentences; when the reaction is a few clips seconds apart, take it as one continuous stretch. The closing
  question (see `story-arc`) comes after them, never inside the script's beats.

## Example (the first candy short)

> What would happen if you put every single taste into one candy? This guy wanted a candy that's sour,
> sweet, salty, bitter, umami, spicy, and icy all at the same time. So he got a sweetener that's twenty
> thousand times sweeter than sugar, pure citric acid, MSG, one of the most bitter chemicals ever
> discovered, and a full gram of pure capsaicin. … But something went wrong. … Somehow, it worked. …
> So he made a second batch with way less spice and even more bitterness.
>
> Then: "It literally is all the taste at the same time." / "It's so sour." / "This is so nasty."
