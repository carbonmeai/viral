# Approach: ranking

**Descriptor:** hook `title-list` · voices `clips-own-sound` · shape `countdown` · language `plain` · ending
`hard-payoff` · format `ranking`.

Drawn from the creator's viral-shorts skill and its final rescues render (`ranking-animal-comebacks.mp4`, 2:39, ten
entries), made after the reference short "Ranking Best Pool Fails" (11.3M views).

**When it fits:** "top N", "ranked", "best…" ideas over several videos found for them, where each video holds one
complete turnaround (a rescue, a restoration, a glow-up). One entry per video. A single video never makes a ranking.

## What it looks like

- A fixed title on top: "Ranking Animal **Comebacks**" — a lead ("Ranking Animal ") and an accent ("Comebacks") drawn
  in red. Keep it short enough for one line (about 32 characters).
- A numbered list down the left, 1 at the top (1 gold, 2 silver, 3 bronze, the last red). As each entry plays, its
  label and emoji appear on its line ("Horse who gave up 🐴") and stay.
- **Played in a shuffled order with #1 last** (7, 10, 5, 9, 4, 8, 6, 3, 2, 1). The viewer waits for #1.
- Full-screen clips, **each with its own sound**, evened out to about −14 LUFS. No music, no voice, no BEFORE/AFTER
  tags, no "#N" cards. (The first version had those; the creator replaced it.)

## Each entry is a mini-story

- For a transformation: **before → being cared for → after.** The care step is required (vet, IV, bath, shave, tube
  feeding, a tractor lift); the creator called it out when it was missing.
- Planned about **3.5 s / 3 s / 4.5 s**; **#1 gets four shots and about 20–25 s** and is the longest entry.
- Shots run longer once their cuts are moved to whole sentences (the rescues ranking planned 125 s and became 159 s,
  +27%), so **plan short** and let the snapping extend them. About 15 s per entry: 10 entries ≈ 2:30–2:40, 5 entries
  ≈ 1:25. Never over 3 minutes (the Shorts limit). A 1-minute 10-entry version "cut too much important part".

## Never cut a sentence in half

Each clip plays its own sound, so every shot's start and end lands on a whole sentence of that video's transcript.
Only a pause over 1.5 s counts as a sentence end, because people pause mid-sentence ("near death … to becoming").
Viral moves each cut to the nearest boundary (at most 3 s back or 4 s forward) and refuses a cut it can't fix: move
the shot instead. Never ship a cut that ends mid-word.

## Look at both ends of every shot

Long shots drift: into another animal, a recap of the whole rescue, or the "before" replayed inside the "after".
**Look at the first and last frame of every shot** and make sure both show the entry's animal in that shot's state.
Videos that fade in from black need the first shot to start at 0.4 s or later. Each shot shows the animal its label
names; a compilation that cuts to other animals is the wrong footage.

## Framing

- Landscape footage plays in a **4:3 window over a blurred copy of itself** (keeps about 75% of the frame; a full 9:16
  crop keeps about 30% and loses the animal).
- Phone footage that is upright **inside** a widescreen file (blurred bars at the sides) **fills the screen**.
- **Never zoom in to hide a video's own subtitles.** The zoomed first version lost the animals; a little burned-in
  text is better.

## Ranking order and labels

- Rank by **how dramatic the turnaround is**, species mixed; the most dramatic is #1.
- Labels are short (about 20 characters), plain and specific: "Lamb learns to run", "Piglet to 600 lbs", "Cotton
  ball dog". One emoji that matches the animal or the feeling (🐴 🐶 🥹).
- Never use excluded ranges: sponsor reads, ads, intro cards, and the end screens and "subscribe" cards in the last
  10–25 seconds of most videos.

## Post text

- Title like "Top 10 Animal Comebacks (Before & After)", under 40 characters, with alternates that tease #1 ("#1 Was
  Lying Down To Die").
- A two-sentence description with the extreme real numbers, no spoiled ending, ending **"Wait for #1."**
- Every channel is credited: a "Footage:" line naming each, and a sources list with each rank's channel and link
  (Viral adds both). No on-screen credit unless the style turns on "via <channel>".

## Thumbnail

"TOP 10" in big yellow letters over the subject ("ANIMAL COMEBACKS"), **#1's after as the hero**, **#1's before as a
tilted inset**. Check the after frame isn't a replay of the before state.
