# Narrated recap

The format comes from a proven viral short: *"He Compressed 100 RedBulls into 1 Drink"* (60s), cut from
*"Making the world's most powerful Red Bull"* (42 min).

- **A new third-person voiceover, not the creator's audio.** "What would happen if you compressed 100 cans
  of Red Bull into one drink? This guy bought 100 cans…". One punchy sentence per story beat, with real
  numbers from the video (25 liters, 150 degrees, 66 cans).
- **It follows the long video in order**, compressing it about 40×: setup → machine → weigh-in → setback
  ("a third evaporated") → result → reaction.
- **The b-roll matches the spoken word.** "Machine" shows the machine, "powder" shows the powder. A new
  shot about every 1.3s.
- **One caption word at a time**, bold and lowercase, with key nouns coloured.
- **It ends on the creator's real reaction with the original audio** ("Whoa, that's crazy. Crazy good."),
  which closes the loop the hook opened.
- No title card: the spoken hook question is the hook.

Arc mapping (see the `story-arc` skill): hook = "What would happen if…?" · rising action = setup and process
· conflict = the setback · comeback = "somehow it worked" / the fix · payoff = the real reaction clip.

## Script rules

- **110–130 words.** That gives about 45s of narration after speeding the voice up; the reaction clip
  brings the short to 50–58s.
- **Line 1 is the open-loop question.** ("What would happen if…?")
- **Line 2 starts "This guy…"** and names the goal in plain words.
- **Every claim and number comes from the transcript.** Never invent.
- **The setback gets a "But…" line**, and the fix gets a "So he…" or "Somehow…" line.
- **Write numbers as words** ("twenty thousand"), so the voice reads them naturally.
- Third person throughout. Short sentences. No filler, no sign-off.

## Shot-list rules

- **Anchor each shot to the voiceover word it illustrates** (a word index plus that word's text), not to
  seconds, so re-voicing or speeding up never breaks the sync.
- **Change shots every 1–2s.** Match each shot to its words.
- **Use the whole frame (`fit`) for on-screen graphics or text** so nothing important is cropped.
- **Mark the hero object** with a circle or arrow at one or two key moments.
- **Never use sponsor, ad, QR or end-screen footage.** Those ranges are excluded and the renderer refuses
  them.
- **End with the creator's real reaction** in their own audio, picked from the transcript and contact
  sheets.
