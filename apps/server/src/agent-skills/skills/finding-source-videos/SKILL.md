# Finding source videos

The creator gave an idea, not videos. You find the videos; Viral downloads them only after the creator approves your
list, then reads each one (transcript, frames) and plans the short from it. Your list is the raw material: a short is
only as good as the moments its videos really contain.

## How to search

- **Split the idea into sub-types and search once per sub-type.** Variety comes from the searches, not from scrolling
  one search. For "animal rescue comebacks": one search per animal ("dog rescue transformation before after",
  "kitten rescue…", "horse…", "donkey…", "pig goat cow…"), plus the conditions ("matted dog…", "emaciated dog…").
  For "over the years": one per decade or era. For fails: one per kind of fail.
- **Then search by channel name** for the channels known for the genre, with the topic: "Hope For Paws rescue
  transformation", "The Dodo pig rescue". Channels that reliably film a whole story: The Dodo, We Love Animals, Hope
  For Paws, Animal Aid Unlimited, Flatbush Cats, Stray Paws of Istanbul (animals); ET, E! News and AP for celebrity
  and news topics.
- **Drop a dead end after two junk searches.** Words like "transformation" pull in film trailers, toys and
  documentaries ("fox bird wild animal rescue transformation" returned Transformers trailers and a Power Rangers
  teaser). When a sub-type returns junk twice, give it up instead of forcing it.
- **Stop once you have enough good candidates**: about two or three strong candidates per video asked for. Searches
  are limited, and every request counts against YouTube's patience.

## What to skip

- **Compilations** ("Top 5 Craziest Transformations", "50 Rescue Cats Before & After"): someone else's edit, cut to
  other subjects mid-shot, often with their own music and captions.
- **Re-uploads, slideshows of photos and machine-made videos.** A channel with 0–5 views on a topic where others have
  millions is usually one of these.
- **Hour-long videos.** At most 20 minutes; prefer under 6. Shorter means less to download and read, and the moments
  are easier to find. A longer one is fine only when nothing shorter tells that story.
- **Live streams** and premieres that haven't happened.

## What makes a good pick

- **One subject, filmed by the people who were there.** The rescue channel's own video of one animal beats a
  compilation that includes it.
- **Every moment the idea needs is on camera.** For a before/after idea, the **before, the care (vet, IV, bath, shave,
  tube feeding, a tractor lift) and the after** must all be filmed; a video that jumps from the rescue to "six months
  later" without the care is weak. Check with `video_details`: chapter titles ("First days at the vet", "A full
  transformation") and the captions' times show where the moments are.
- **Each pick brings something the others don't.** Mix the sub-types (species, decades, kinds of fail); two dogs with
  the same story is one too many.
- **Order** the picks the way the short plays them. For a ranking, order by how dramatic the turnaround is, with #1
  last; give each pick its part ("#3: the matted dog").
- **Views are a hint, not a rule.** A 49k-view piglet with the right story beats an 8M-view video that doesn't fit.

## Moments and times

For each pick, list the moments you expect with rough times in seconds: before, care and after for a transformation;
the build and the payoff for a fail. They are guesses from the captions and chapters, and Viral says so; give null
when you can't tell rather than inventing a time. Start each label with the moment's name and a colon, then a few
words ("before: matted and scared", "care: the first bath", "after: playing at home"): the shortlist shows the name
as a chip and the rest on hover. Don't stretch a video's description into moments it doesn't
mention.

## Backups

Add up to 3 backups: good candidates that could take a spot if a pick can't be downloaded or turns out not to have
its moments (a careful look at downloaded videos still drops about one in four). Prefer backups of a different
sub-type from each other.

## Rules

- Never invent a video, a title, a channel or a time. Pick only videos your searches or lookups returned.
- Never pick a removed, private or blocked video. If YouTube refuses a request, stop searching and propose from what
  you already found; never try to get around a block.
- Each video goes on the list once, even under two different links.
