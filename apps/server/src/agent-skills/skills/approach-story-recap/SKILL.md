# Approach: story recap

**Descriptor:** hook `claim` or `question` · voices `interleaved` · shape `full-story` · language `plain` · ending
`real-payoff` · format `narrated`.

Taken from the creator's best-performing short, "He Said He's Not Scared of Anything" (113 s), cut from a Nick
DiGiovanni video: Chef Rush against ten levels of spicy food, ending on Pepper X. It is
`claim-interleaved` run over the whole video, longer, with a long real ending.

**When it fits:** a video with clear stages (levels, rounds, attempts, days) and a person who reacts all the way
through: challenges, food, experiments, builds, transformations. It needs enough stages and real lines to fill
60–120 s without padding; if the video has three stages and one good reaction, prefer `claim-interleaved` or
`question-recap`.

## Shape

Narration and real audio flow as **one conversation**: the narrator sets up each moment and the real person delivers
the punchline in their own voice.

```
NARRATOR  the boast or open question         → REAL  the person saying it themselves
NARRATOR  the rules and the stakes
NARRATOR  stage 1, stage 2, stage 3… one punchy line each, with its real number
NARRATOR  sets up a moment                   → REAL  the person's answer
NARRATOR  "But…" the setback                 → REAL  the pain, the confession
NARRATOR  "So he… / Somehow…" the fix
NARRATOR  the last stage, the biggest number → REAL  "And you want me to eat it?"
NARRATOR  the final set-up
REAL      the payoff: ONE continuous real take, 20 s or more
```

## Script rules

- **Map every stage first.** List all of them (all ten levels), the rules and stakes, the setbacks, and the 6–10 best
  real lines. **Cover the full story**: a highlight reel loses viewers.
- **150–220 narrated words** (60–90 s of narration); with the real lines the short runs 60–120 s.
- **Open with the boast or the open question**: "This man says he's not scared of anything."
- **Then the rules and stakes** in a line or two ("This jug of milk is his health bar. Finish it before level ten and
  he loses."), then **each stage in one punchy line** with its real number.
- **Write the set-up the person answers** before each real line, so the real line closes it.
- **Numbers as words** ("five hundred thirty-eight"), third person, short sentences. Every fact from the transcript.

## Real lines

- **5–8 inserts during the narration**, each a whole sentence or two, up to about 7 s.
- **The payoff is one continuous real take** of the ending (spicy: 22.6 s), merging clips a few seconds apart so no
  line is skipped.
- A real line may say what the narrator was about to say; then the narration picks up after it rather than repeating
  it (three of the spicy short's seven inserts did this).
- Whole sentences only (a pause over 1.5 s ends one), clean audio, never in an excluded range.
- After the payoff comes the closing question ("What's the hottest pepper you've ever eaten?", see `story-arc`).

## Example (the spicy short's narration, about 200 words)

Lines in [brackets] were voiced but skipped, because the real line right before them says the same thing.

> This man says he's not scared of anything. → REAL "I am not scared of anything."
> So a chef cooked him ten levels of spicy food, ending with the hottest pepper on earth. The rules were simple.
> This jug of milk is his health bar. Finish it before level ten and he loses. Level one was a bell pepper, zero
> heat. Level two, shishito sushi. Level three, a jalapeño hot dog, and he ate the whole raw jalapeño. Then things
> got serious. Level five was crying tiger beef with Thai chilies twenty times hotter than a jalapeño.
> → REAL "It's like getting electrocuted all in front of my mouth." [He said it felt like getting electrocuted.]
> At level six, the chef handed him a cooling sauce. → REAL "Wait. That's not cooling. It's not cooling. Yeah, I
> lied." [It was just as spicy. He lied.] By level eight, half the milk was gone. This curry used ghost peppers so
> strong the Indian army made grenades out of them. And when the chef wasn't listening, he whispered,
> → REAL "I'm not going to say this in front of Nick. This is really hard. Do not tell him." [this is really hard,
> don't tell him.] Level nine was Carolina Reaper stingray. A medic waited off camera, and he started crying.
> → REAL "But that heat is crazy. It's crazy." And level ten wasn't even a dish. It was Pepper X, five hundred
> thirty-eight jalapeños in one bite. → REAL "And you want me to eat it?" He signed a waiver, and ate it.
> → REAL "My heart is beating out of my chest right now." He survived. So he asked for something to cool down. The
> chef gave him ice cream, spiked with over twenty million Scoville.
> → REAL the payoff, one 22.6 s take: the ice cream, and all the milk.
